NovaPay Internal Audit and Policy Framework Development

This project focuses on developing a structured policy framework for NovaPay as part of an internal audit and policy framework assignment. It establishes a two-tier policy hierarchy consisting of foundational and advanced policies, and demonstrates the policy life cycle through the development of a Change Management Policy for Solstice Information Security & Technology Governance.

Project Overview
The project examines policy organization and development within an information security and technology governance framework. Its primary objectives are to establish a clear policy hierarchy, distinguish foundational policies from specialized policies, and demonstrate how an organizational policy can be developed and managed throughout its life cycle.

The assignment is structured around two main components:

1. Policy Hierarchy

A two-tier policy framework was developed to organize Solstice's information security and governance policies according to their purpose and level of specialization.

Tier 1: Foundational Policies

These policies establish the fundamental security and governance requirements applicable across the organization.
- Information Security Policy: Defines the overall direction and standards for protecting the organization's technology and information assets.
- Access Control Policy: Establishes how authorized users are granted, controlled, reviewed, and restricted access to systems and information.
- Acceptable Use Policy: Defines guidelines for the appropriate and responsible use of technology resources and information systems.
- Incident Response Policy: Provides organizational guidance for identifying, documenting, managing, and responding to security incidents.
Tier 2: Advanced Policies

These policies address specialized operations that require more detailed risk assessment, approval, monitoring, and coordination.
- Change Management Policy: Establishes a controlled process for requesting, assessing, approving, implementing, and reviewing changes to technology and systems.
- Third-Party Risk Management Policy: Defines standards for identifying, evaluating, and managing security and compliance risks associated with third-party service providers.
2. Selected Policy for Life-Cycle Application

The Change Management Policy was selected to demonstrate the policy development and implementation life cycle.

The policy establishes a structured process for managing changes to Solstice's infrastructure, applications, information systems, and security measures.

Its life cycle follows five stages:
1. Request
2. Assess
3. Approve
4. Implement
5. Review

Configuration Steps
The following steps outline the policy framework development and Change Management Policy life cycle presented in the assignment.

1. Establish the Policy Hierarchy
- Identify the information security and technology governance policies required by the organization.
- Organize policies into two levels: foundational and advanced.
- Classify Information Security, Access Control, Acceptable Use, and Incident Response as foundational policies.
- Classify Change Management and Third-Party Risk Management as advanced policies.
2. Define the Policy Framework Rationale
- Establish foundational policies as the basis for organizational security and governance.
- Position specialized policies in the advanced tier because they require more detailed risk assessment, approval, monitoring, and coordination across business and technology functions.
3. Select a Policy for Life-Cycle Application
- Select the Change Management Policy to demonstrate the policy life cycle.
- Establish the policy under Solstice Information Security & Technology Governance.
- Identify the policy owner as IT Governance and Information Security.
- Identify the Chief Information Officer (CIO) as the approval authority.
- Establish an annual minimum review period.
- Set the policy status as Proposed.
4. Define the Policy Purpose and Scope
- Establish the purpose of controlling changes to infrastructure, applications, information systems, and security measures.
- Aim to minimize service interruptions, security issues, errors, and unauthorized changes.
- Apply the policy to employees, contractors, and other authorized personnel involved in requesting, evaluating, approving, implementing, or reviewing changes.
- Cover planned, standard, and emergency changes that may affect information, applications, infrastructure, security, system availability, or business operations.
5. Establish the Change Request Process
- Require all proposed changes to be formally requested and recorded before execution.
- Document the reason for each change.
- Identify affected systems and services.
- Record anticipated impacts and relevant risks.
6. Establish Assessment and Approval Requirements
- Evaluate proposed changes for business, security, operational, and service impacts.
- Require approval from appropriate authorized personnel before implementation.
- Obtain suitable management approval for significant or higher-risk changes.
7. Establish Implementation and Review Requirements
- Implement approved changes according to a documented change plan.
- Conduct testing before deployment where necessary.
- Review completed changes to confirm that expected outcomes were achieved.
- Identify unforeseen operational, security, or service problems resulting from implementation.
8. Define Policy Approval and Review Arrangements
- Require approval from the Chief Information Officer (CIO), with input from Information Security and relevant business owners.
- Review the policy at least once annually.
- Allow earlier revisions when significant changes occur in organizational operations, the technical environment, legal requirements, or the organization's risk profile.
9. Apply the Complete Policy Life Cycle
- Request: Formally document proposed changes.
- Assess: Evaluate potential risks and impacts.
- Approve: Obtain authorization from the appropriate personnel.
- Implement: Execute approved changes under controlled conditions.
- Review: Evaluate the outcome and identify any unexpected issues.
Results and Findings
The project established a two-tier policy hierarchy and developed a proposed Change Management Policy to demonstrate how organizational policies can be structured and applied.

Key results:
- Structured Policy Hierarchy: Established foundational and advanced policy categories to organize information security and technology governance requirements.
- Foundational Governance: Identified four policies covering information security, access control, acceptable use, and incident response.
- Specialized Policy Controls: Identified change management and third-party risk management as advanced policies requiring more specialized oversight.
- Documented Change Management Policy: Developed a proposed policy defining its purpose, scope, ownership, approval authority, review period, and key requirements.
- Defined Accountability: Assigned policy ownership to IT Governance and Information Security and approval authority to the Chief Information Officer.
- Controlled Change Process: Established a five-stage policy life cycle covering request, assessment, approval, implementation, and review.
- Risk-Based Decision-Making: Incorporated business, security, operational, and service impact assessments before changes are approved.
- Continuous Policy Review: Established an annual minimum review requirement, with provisions for earlier reviews when significant organizational, technical, legal, or risk-related changes occur.
Key findings:

The policy hierarchy provides a structured foundation for organizing organizational security requirements before introducing more specialized controls. The Change Management Policy further demonstrates the importance of documenting proposed changes, evaluating associated risks, obtaining appropriate authorization, implementing changes under controlled conditions, and reviewing their outcomes.

The project highlights how clearly defined policy ownership, approval authority, responsibilities, and review requirements contribute to stronger information security and technology governance.

Overall, the assignment provides a practical foundation for policy framework development and demonstrates how a structured policy life cycle can support accountability, risk management, and controlled technology operations.
